The references below identify the methods used, without claiming that independent exposition establishes theoretical priority. ## 2. Dual evaluation and discrete cash events

[Haugh and Kogan (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.1030.0070), *Pricing American Options: A Duality Approach*, *Operations Research*, 52(2), 258–270, supplies the martingale upper-bound principle and approximate-value construction. I consulted the published author-hosted journal PDF, principally Sections 3.1–3.3 and the opening value-iteration identities in Section 4.1, journal pages 260–262. The historical construction centres piecewise-linear values analytically within the maintained transition model. It does not reproduce the paper's numerical experiments. [Rogers (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9965.02010), *Monte Carlo valuation of American options*, *Mathematical Finance*, 12(3), 271–286, is acknowledged as an independent duality contribution; the recorded consultation of that article is limited to publisher metadata and abstract.

For Longstaff and Schwartz (2001), I consulted the [ETH-hosted published journal scan](https://people.math.ethz.ch/~hjfurrer/teaching/LongstaffSchwartzAmericanOptionsLeastSquareMonteCarlo.pdf), including the realised-cash example, Section 2.2 and Sections 8.2–8.3 on regression and basis conditioning. The independent implementation uses their backward continuation-regression principle. It does not claim that the chosen basis removes approximation error.

[Vellekoop and Nieuwenhuis (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1080/13504860600563077), *Efficient Pricing of Derivatives on Assets with Discrete Dividends*, *Applied Mathematical Finance*, 13(3), 265–284, supplies the model-comparison context for discrete cash events. I consulted its [institutional publication record](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/efficient-pricing-of-derivatives-on-assets-with-discrete-dividend/) and text extracted from the linked institutional PDF, principally the introduction and opening model discussion in Section 2. This was not a visual inspection of the publisher PDF or a full-paper review. The funded receivable model in the paper states its own cash cap and timing assumptions; citation does not establish that they reproduce listed-contract entitlements.

The comparison with [Andersen and Broadie (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.1040.0258), [Belomestny, Bender and Schoenmakers (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9965.2008.00357.x) and [Cline (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1080/14697688.2026.2647986) identifies established primal-dual, non-nested and regression-based constructions. The retained consultation records distinguish passages read from bibliographic attribution. I have not implemented their complete algorithms as comparative benchmarks. The paper's [bibliographic file](paper/references.bib) gives their journal details.

## 3. Allocation, cost and inference

[Glynn and Whitt (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.40.3.505), *The asymptotic efficiency of simulation estimators*, *Operations Research*, 40(3), 505–520, provides context for assessing simulation error jointly with stochastic computational cost. I consulted journal pages 505–507 in the published scan and the author abstract. I did not consult the later random-time-change proof for this study. The paper's fixed-count conditioning argument and finite cost-stopping example are independently written applications of conditional expectation and optional sampling, without a priority claim.

[Fieller (1954)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1954.tb00159.x) and [von Luxburg and Franz (2009)](https://www3.stat.sinica.edu.tw/sstest/j19n3/j19n312/j19n312.html) supply the ratio-inference foundations. Fieller was consulted through the publisher abstract and extracted text of journal pages 175–177, equations (5)–(10), without a retained local PDF or visual inspection. For von Luxburg and Franz, I consulted the final published PDF, pages 1095–1108, including equation (2.1), Construction 2 and Theorem 5; pages 1102–1103 were inspected visually. The historical paper distinguishes exact normal-model Fieller inference from its asymptotic joint-moment inversion. Appendix C of the [historical paper](paper/Exercise_Policy_Loss_under_Computational_and_Financial_Constraints.pdf) derives the component map, endpoint allocation comparison and variance-contrast standard error.

[Hoeffding (1963)](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830), *Probability Inequalities for Sums of Bounded Random Variables*, *Journal of the American Statistical Association*, 58(301), 13–30, underlies the auxiliary bounded-component calculations. I consulted the published scan, including Theorem 2, equation (2.6), the exponential-moment argument in Section 4 and the bounded-kernel inequality in Section 5a, equation (5.7). The sample-variance application uses the order-two U-statistic result in Section 5a, not Theorem 7. These fixed-sample inequalities do not provide time-uniform coverage or a CPU guarantee.

[Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe and Sekhon (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1214/20-AOS1991), *Time-uniform, nonparametric, nonasymptotic confidence sequences*, *The Annals of Statistics*, 49(2), 1055–1080, supplies the auxiliary sequential-inference foundations. The consulted published passages are Section 2, Definition 1 and equation (2.2); Section 3.1, Theorem 1 and equation (3.1); Section 3.2, Lemma 2 and equation (3.7); and Section 4.1, Theorem 4 and equation (4.1). I did not review the complete supplementary proofs. These diagnostics require their stated support, predictability and supermartingale assumptions. They are distinct from the approximate inferential comparisons reported in the completed paper and do not establish its financial inputs or expenditure forecasts.

## 6. Data and figures

The baseline numerical validation and the public replication fixture use stated mathematical inputs and recorded pseudorandom seeds. They are not market observations. The historical paper separately reports conditional simulation findings from retained financial specifications. Public findings and public fixtures have different evidential roles: running a stipulated fixture checks the implementation but does not reproduce the paper's licensed-input experiment. The [replication guide](replication/README.md) states the distinction and the requirements for independently supplied inputs. External articles are linked, not redistributed.

The historical financial specifications and September observation-admission analysis use LSEG Workspace, accessed through the University of St Andrews subscription; the reported numerical results are my calculations. The conditional specification uses observations from a short intraday window on 10 September 2026, while the separate admission exercise concerns 21–25 September. These are observation periods, not asserted retrieval dates. Acquisition records establish the retrieval vintage. Current public-model simulations use stated mathematical parameters and have separate provenance. The [publication policy](docs/publication_policy.md) applies the quantity, distribution, commercial-value, attribution and retention limits to each LSEG-dependent output.

The public Treasury rate snapshot comes from the [US Treasury's documented feed](https://home.treasury.gov/treasury-daily-interest-rate-xml-feed). Each saved snapshot includes the original XML, parsed observations, observation cutoff, retrieval time and source hash. See [the data plan](paper/data_admission.md) for the distinction between published par yields and the eventual pricing curve, and for the input requirements of an independent market-validation extension. The attempted original observation-admission exercise yielded no admissible primary observations; that limitation is stated in the historical paper.

The [Cboe Option EOD Summary product page](https://datashop.cboe.com/option-eod-summary), its [file specification](https://datashop.cboe.com/documents/Option_EOD_Summary_Layout.pdf) and its [published sample archive](https://datashop.cboe.com/download/sample/217) support the [format audit](paper/data_admission.md). The sample is provider demonstration material dated 25 August 2023, not the project's recent empirical dataset. Its raw files and the specification are stored privately with hashes. Provider field labels are reproduced where necessary to identify columns exactly; explanatory text is original.

## 7. Scope of the contribution

The historical contribution is a conditional computational study of policy loss, evaluation slack, training and evaluation allocation, and CPU prediction under a stated funded stopping model. Its controlled comparisons retain both favourable precision findings and unsuccessful cost forecasts. They do not establish market profitability, a universal allocation rule or a validated independent market-pricing result. The independently written derivations explain how the comparisons identify their stated quantities; they do not establish theoretical priority.

The historical PDF and its public numerical summaries record the released findings; the [paper guide](paper/README.md) identifies the current revision. Restricted source observations, fitted financial inputs and private working records remain excluded under the [publication policy](docs/publication_policy.md). The [licensing scope](LICENSING.md) and [third-party notices](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md) distinguish the repository's writing and software licences from the rights attached to external data, dependencies and publications.
